        BaZi is a traditional Chinese system. These readings describe tendencies and themes to reflect on. They are not predictions or facts about you, and shouldn't drive big decisions on their own.

        Enter a birth date on the Chart tab.

        How to read a BaZi chart
        1. Start with the day master (the Day stem). It stands for you; everything else is read against it.
        2. Look at the ten gods. Each stem is a relationship to you: what supports you (Resource), what you create (Output), what you earn (Wealth), what disciplines you (Officer) and who stands beside you (Companion).
        3. Note where they sit. Year = roots and childhood, Month = parents and work, Day branch = your partner, Hour = children and later life.
        4. Check the balance. Too much or too little of an element or theme shows where life tends to feel easy or effortful.
        5. Add timing. Each 10-year luck pillar and each year brings new ten gods that switch different themes on, and clashes often mark change.

          About Zi Wei Dou Shu
          • Zi Wei Dou Shu (“Purple Star Astrology”) places 14 main stars and many minor ones in 12 palaces, each a part of life, from the lunar date and two-hour period of birth. It's a separate system from BaZi; people often use both: BaZi for balance and timing, Zi Wei for the detail of each life area.
          • It uses exactly the same birth moment as the BaZi chart: time zone, true solar time and the 23:00 rule all apply.
          • Method: the common 紫微斗数全书 rules. The year changes at the lunar new year; a leap-month birth counts as that month to the 15th and the next month from the 16th; 庚 years transform 太阳, 武曲, 太阴, 天同.
          • Checked against the open-source iztro library on nearly 5,000 random charts, with identical results. Star brightness (庙旺利陷) isn't shown yet.

            About

            • The year changes at 立春 (Start of Spring, about 4 February) and each month at its solar term, not on the lunar calendar.
            • Solar terms are worked out from the sun's position on your device and agree with published times to within a minute.
            • Lunar dates are converted using the official rules (new moons and solar terms in Beijing time), for 1901–2099.
